# In class

## What we will do

1. Introduce the course and one another.
2. Distinguish AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI.
3. Sign in to Icerynk with your NYU Google account and confirm you can reach the live class connection. **Answering a poll does not require signing in** — you scan the code on the projector and answer anonymously — so a sign-in problem never keeps you out of the room's work.
4. Sit on both sides of an airline support desk in **The Turing Desk**, then meet the real case it is built from.
5. Use live polls to separate documented facts from guesses, choose one repair, and make the Monday-morning operating decision — then compare it with the decision you made before you investigated anything.
6. Name the work you just did: claim, source of truth and grounding, failure hypothesis, controlled test, review gate and escalation, operating boundary.
7. Read the model market at **openrouter.ai/models** — what a model card tells you, why input and output are priced separately, what the modality tabs reveal about supply, and which two filters actually decide a deployment.
8. Spend six minutes finding a model yourself that meets three stated constraints, and answer the harder question: *would you deploy it, and what do you still not know?*
9. Watch two models answer the same prompt side by side in **openrouter.ai/chat**, and see why that is an impression rather than a controlled test.

The class deck is a separate course material. Use this page to find the work and required output.

## Bring

A charged laptop and a browser, and have your phone on you for the live polls. That is the whole list: nothing to install, and no account beyond Icerynk required to walk in the door.

## Leave with

- One judgment you made under time pressure, and the moment you found out whether it held up.
- One judgment you had to make in the moment: whether an answer you were given was actually supported by the policy behind it.
- Four facts written down about one model you found yourself: **name, provider, input price, output price** — plus at least one honest gap in what the page told you.
- A clear picture of Project 1: done in pairs, browser only, free models allowed, **presented in three minutes at the start of Friday July 31** with the evidence uploaded to **Brightspace** as one PDF or zip beforehand.
- One task for lunch: a **free OpenRouter account**, with the Chat page open and one model added.

## Two things people get wrong

**"Free" still requires a signup.** Sending a prompt without an account triggers a signup modal. Free means no payment, not no account.

**The playground is a workbench, not an archive.** Anything you want to keep — a prompt, a model's raw output — has to be pasted into your own document as you go. A refreshed tab or a switched model can take it with it. This matters from your very first Project 1 case.
