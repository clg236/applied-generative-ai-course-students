## 15-Minute Simulation: Adjusting a Yasso 800 Workout

**Goal:** Practice making safe training decisions when tired, experiencing mild pain, and running in hot, humid conditions.

**Scenario:** You planned 8 × 800 meters at your Yasso pace, with an easy jog of equal duration between repetitions. Today, however:

- You slept poorly and feel unusually tired.
- You have mild pain in your left calf.
- It is hot and humid.
- You still want to preserve the workout’s aerobic purpose without turning it into a harmful session.

### Minute 0–2: Pre-run assessment

Rate each factor:

- Fatigue: 1–10
- Pain: 1–10
- Heat discomfort: 1–10

Check the pain:

- Is it localized or general soreness?
- Does it affect walking or your normal running stride?
- Is it sharp, worsening, or associated with swelling?
- Does it improve or worsen during gentle movement?

**Decision point:** If the pain is sharp, changes your stride, worsens while walking, or is accompanied by swelling, stop the simulation and replace the workout with rest or medical evaluation. Do not use interval training to “test” an injury.

If the pain is mild, stable, and does not affect movement, continue to a cautious warm-up.

### Minute 2–5: Modified warm-up

Simulate:

- 2 minutes of easy jogging
- 30 seconds of walking
- 30 seconds of gentle mobility for the ankles, calves, and hips

Reassess fatigue, pain, and heat discomfort.

**Runner prompt:** “My calf pain began at 2/10 and is now 3/10, while my stride feels slightly different. What should I do?”

**Best response:** Stop the Yasso workout. A rising pain score plus altered mechanics indicates that fast repetitions are inappropriate. Choose easy pain-free movement or rest instead.

If pain remains at 1–2/10, does not increase, and stride stays normal, proceed with a heavily adjusted workout.

### Minute 5–7: Choose the adjustment

The original workout is **8 × 800 meters at Yasso pace**. Because fatigue and heat both increase physiological strain, do not try to preserve the original pace, repetition count, and recovery structure simultaneously.

Choose one of these safer options:

**Option A: Reduced Yasso session**

- 4–5 × 800 meters instead of 8
- Run by controlled effort, not exact target pace
- Aim for approximately 6–7/10 effort
- Slow the pace as needed
- Extend recovery with easy jogging or walking
- Stop if pace deteriorates sharply or pain increases

**Option B: Time-based intervals**

- 4–5 repetitions of 3–4 minutes
- Comfortable, controlled aerobic effort
- 2–3 minutes of easy recovery
- Ignore normal Yasso pace because heat and humidity make pace misleading

**Option C: Easy-run replacement**

- 20–40 minutes at conversational effort
- Appropriate if fatigue is substantial or the calf remains questionable
- The missed Yasso workout is not rescheduled immediately

**Best choice for this scenario:** Option B or C. Mild pain, fatigue, heat, and humidity together justify a conservative adjustment.

1. Is my breathing controlled?
2. Is my stride normal?

**Repetition 1:** Pace is slower than normal, effort is 6/10, and pain remains 2/10.

**Decision:** Continue, but keep the slower pace.

**Repetition 2:** Effort rises to 7/10 because of the humidity. Pain remains stable.

**Decision:** Extend recovery and reduce the next interval’s intensity. Do not chase the planned pace.

**Repetition 3:** Pain increases to 4/10 and you begin shortening your stride.

**Decision:** Stop the workout immediately and walk to cool down. Do not complete the final repetition.

If pain had stayed stable and effort remained controlled, you could complete the fourth repetition, but adding extra intervals would still be unnecessary.

### Minute 11–13: Heat-management response

Before and during the session:

- Choose a shaded route, track, or treadmill if available.
- Train during the coolest part of the day.
- Drink fluids according to thirst and your established hydration plan.
- Use light, breathable clothing.
- Allow a slower pace and longer recovery.
- End the session for dizziness, confusion, faintness, chills, severe headache, vomiting, or loss of coordination.

**Key lesson:** In heat and humidity, effort and symptoms matter more than matching a pace calculated under cooler conditions.

Complete these statements:
