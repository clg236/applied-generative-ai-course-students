# Field Evidence Sprint

Work in pairs. Go out into the built environment around NYU and look for one small opportunity to make New York a better place to live and work.

Start with something a person is already trying to do. Try it yourselves. Work out what information or capability is missing before you say anything about AI. Bring back an idea specific enough that someone else can challenge it.

**Field mission: 3:30–4:15 PM · Stop collecting and begin returning at 4:05 · Both partners seated by 4:15**

You need a phone, internet access, and your partner. You do not need your laptop in the field.

## Try the task yourself

Look for a repeated task made harder because the information or capability is not good enough.

- Follow a service-change notice or check a public permit.
- Observe a repeated public task without disturbing the people doing it.
- Watch a crossing or traffic pattern from a safe public place.

Do not start with “Where could we add AI?” Start with what a person is already trying to do.

## Bring back an idea we can prove wrong

Draft this sentence:

> Given **[input]**, produce **[output]**, so **[person]** can **[do something]**.

Then ask:

> Can we write the correct answer for one case before starting the formal runs?

If not, narrow the task.

## Actively try things out

- Use your phones, the internet, and AI to explore.
- Treat a field AI trial as an informal experiment, not one of the three formal Project 1 runs.
- Track where you stop, guess, or repeat work.
- Notice what information you need.

An informal model output is not an observation and does not prove that the idea works. Keep the prompt and output, but the baseline, Eval A, and Eval B begin only after the six-case answer key is fixed.

## Bring back four lines

1. **What you saw**
2. **Who it makes life harder for**
3. **What they have to do about it now**
4. **What information or capability is missing** — do not say AI yet

Then ask whether you can write the correct answer before running anything. If not, narrow the problem.

## Use your phone like a field notebook

- Take one **wide** photo for context and one **tight** photo for readable detail when photos help.
- Make notes.
- Audio or video is optional; collect it only when it is safe and permissioned.
- Record what is missing as carefully as what is present.

Identifying media stays on your device. A privacy-safe photo, audio clip, or video may be part of the later test only after both partners review it against the rules below.

## Obvious but important

- Stay with your partner in public or permitted NYU space.
- No faces, names, badges, plates, addresses, private screens, or private belongings.
- Do not record conversations without permission.
- Do not block or interfere with a person, queue, doorway, or work area.
- If anyone asks you to stop, stop and walk away.

Do not make another person the subject of the exercise. A hosted model is somebody else’s computer; there is no unsend.

## The walk back is part of the 45 minutes

At **4:05 PM**, stop collecting wherever you are and begin returning. Be seated in KMC 2-70 with your partner by **4:15 PM**, even if the packet is incomplete.

Stay inside the search box bounded by **W 4th St, Broadway, W 3rd St, and Sixth Ave**. When the indoor alternative is called, use **KMC floors 1–2 and Kimmel floors 1–4** under the same evidence and privacy rules. An incomplete safe packet is better than an unsafe or invented one.

## Before you turn back, write the four lines

Complete these first:

1. **What you saw**
2. **Who it makes life harder for**
3. **What they have to do about it now**
4. **What information or capability is missing**

Then draft one task sentence and one correct answer. The idea can change when you get back. It only needs to be specific enough for someone else to challenge.

## Separate what you saw from what you inferred

Open one shared document. Label every evidence claim:

| Label | Put here |
|---|---|
| **Observed** | exact words, time, sequence, count, or visible condition |
| **Interpreted** | likely user, possible cause, or possible effect |
| **Unknown** | missing rule, missing source, or unanswered question |

Mark each line **O**, **I**, or **?** Remove identifying details. Any AI trial remains separate from what you directly observed.

## Write the smallest testable version

> Given **[input]**, produce **[output]**, so **[person]** can **[act]**.

Then finish:

- **The input comes from...**
- **We know it is correct when...**
- **If the answer is missing, the system must say...**
- **The output is checked by... before...**
- **Without AI, we would...**

Do not start the formal Project 1 runs yet. Write the test first. “I can't tell from this” is correct when the evidence does not contain the answer; a confident guess is a failure.

## Talk to another pair

1. Explain the idea and the task.
2. The other pair gives you:
   - one ordinary test case;
   - one case where the answer should be **“can't tell”**; and
   - one simpler non-AI fix.
3. Write down the strongest challenge. Do not defend the pitch.
4. Switch directions so both pairs receive a challenge.

## Revise once and build the hand-in

- Record the challenge you received.
- Revise the task, answer rule, or non-AI comparison once.
- Set the next time both partners will work.
- Start one shared document with the hand-in checklist:

1. The four field lines
2. The task sentence, workflow, and non-AI alternative
3. Six cases with the answer key written first
4. Baseline, Eval A, and Eval B results
5. The cost trade-off table
6. Every raw output as text

Both partners keep a copy and schedule the next work time. This is your Project 1 pair.
